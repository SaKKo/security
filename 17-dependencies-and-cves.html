17 · Dependencies and CVEs

An app pulls in hundreds of libraries, all running with full power. When one gets a known hole, attackers start scanning within days.

Nobody updates your dependencies for you

When a library ships a security fix, nothing changes in our app until we update. Until then, the lockfile keeps installing the old, vulnerable version.

Run bundler-audit and brakeman, turn on Dependabot, and stay on a Rails version that still gets security fixes.

CVEs and advisories, briefly

  • A CVE is just an ID for one known vulnerability.
  • Advisory databases say which versions are affected.
  • Severity scores help, but what matters is whether we actually use the broken code.

Updating safely: change as little as possible

Update just the broken package, not everything. A small change ships today; a big one turns into a week of fixing unrelated breakage.

A dependency hygiene checklist


    

  • Commit the lockfile.
    • 

  • Automate it: Dependabot plus an audit step in CI.
    • 

  • Follow Rails security announcements.
    • 

  • Update little and often. A year behind is hard to patch in a hurry.
    • 

  • Be picky about new gems. Watch for look-alike names.
    • 

  • Install from the lockfile in CI and production.
